EchoSight

See the conversation. Join the conversation. Keep the conversation.

EchoSight is a Deaf-first communication assistant for iPhone. Live captions, two-way face-to-face conversation, and saved transcripts, all processed on your phone. No account needed. Nothing leaves your device unless you choose a Cloud lens for one session, and the app tells you exactly what is sent.

Why I built this

Hi, I'm Frank. I'm Deaf. Like a lot of us, my phone is full of apps that each do one piece of a conversation: one app captions, another one speaks for me, a third one keeps notes. Switching between them in the middle of a real conversation, whether at a counter, in a meeting, or at a doctor's office, means missing the conversation itself.

EchoSight puts the whole conversation on one screen: what they're saying, what I want to say back, and a saved record I can return to later. It works with no internet connection, and everything stays on the phone.

It's in active beta testing now, shaped directly by feedback from Deaf and hard-of-hearing testers. If that's you, or someone you talk with, I'd love your help making it better.

What it does today

Meeting Mode: meetings, classes, appointments

(Being renamed "Echo Mode" in an upcoming update.)

  • Live captions of everything said, saved as a full-session transcript you can scroll back through and export
  • Survives interruptions: phone calls, Siri, switching earbuds. Captions come back on their own, and if anything stalls you see a banner, never silence
  • Name alerts: your phone vibrates when your name is spoken
  • Audio status at a glance: Bluetooth mic connected, background noise high, poor audio, plus placement tips for best results
  • AI wrap-up on supported iPhones: summary, action items, and decisions, generated on your device, and flagged when the audio was too poor to trust
  • Lenses: turn any saved session into a study guide, quiz, vocabulary list, action items, decisions, or a "what did I miss?" catch-up. Every item links back to the transcript lines it came from

Conversation Mode: face to face

  • Their words captioned on top, your side in big text below. One screen, two directions
  • Face them: one tap flips your side so the person across the table reads it without you turning the phone around
  • Type or tap a quick phrase; EchoSight can speak it out loud in a natural voice, at the speed you choose
  • Smart reply suggestions when someone asks you a question, including the actual choices when it's a "this or that?" question
  • Quick-phrase packs for restaurants, medical visits, work, travel, and emergencies, plus phrases it learns you use often
  • Volume stays in your control: EchoSight never changes your volume by itself

EchoSight supports communication access. It is not a replacement for a qualified interpreter or CART services, and it will always say so.

Feature tour: everything, including the easy-to-miss

The in-app tutorial is short on purpose, which means some of the best features are ones you'd only find by poking around. This is the complete list of what's in your hands today. If something here surprises you, that's exactly the feedback I want.

In Conversation Mode

  • Big text, both directions: their words captioned live on top, your side displayed large below. One screen, no app switching
  • Face them: the flip button turns your side of the screen to face the person across the table, so they read while you keep the phone. Tap your text to flip it back and keep typing
  • Speak or show: every message can display silently or be spoken aloud in a natural voice. A chip under the Send button always shows which will happen; tap it to switch
  • See the speech as it happens: when EchoSight speaks for you, a word-by-word highlight shows exactly where the voice is in your sentence, so you know when it's done
  • Reply ideas: when someone asks you a question, tap the sparkles for suggested replies, generated on your phone
  • The wand: optional writing help that rewords your draft into natural conversational English for a hearing listener: your meaning, your tone, smoother delivery. On your phone, off by default
  • Quick phrases and scenario packs: saved one-tap messages, with ready-made packs for restaurants, medical visits, errands, work, travel, and emergencies. Send the same message three times and EchoSight offers to save it
  • Long-press any bubble to speak it again (they didn't hear it) or copy it
  • Tools for the hearing person under the person icon: an on-screen explanation of the app, a "please pause while I read" banner, and "Let them type": hand the phone over and their typed words join their side of the transcript
  • Volume and mic status, honestly shown: a banner with a real slider if your volume is too low to be heard, and a clear "mic paused while speaking" vs "still listening" state. EchoSight never changes your volume by itself

In Meeting Mode

  • Captions that go the distance: continuous on-device transcription, validated on a three-hour unbroken session: over 32,000 words, one transcript
  • Caption speed, your choice: Fast shows words the instant they're recognized and cleans them up in place; Steady waits until text is settled so it never changes under your eyes
  • Gray means live, white means done: words appear in gray the instant they're recognized and may adjust slightly; when a sentence turns white, it's final. That gray→white flip is by design, so you always know which words are still settling
  • "Hearing you" indicator: a live mic-level animation, so a quiet room never looks like a dead app
  • Scroll back freely: reading history never stops the captions; a jump-to-live button brings you back
  • Name alerts: a strong vibration when your name is spoken, including the ways people mispronounce it (you teach it the variants)
  • AI wrap-up: summary, key points, action items, and decisions at session end, or generate one later from History. Made on your phone, never uploaded
  • Wrap-ups that admit their limits: if the audio was poor for much of the session, the wrap-up says so at the top and shows action items and decisions as said rather than reworded. Numbers are kept only when they appear in the transcript, in the sentence they came from. Lines the phone couldn't hear well are marked [unclear]
  • Notes at the moment: tap Add a note at this moment while a meeting runs, type your thought, and it lands in the transcript exactly where you tapped, in orange, even if people kept talking while you typed. Afterward, touch and hold any line in a saved session to add a note after it. Notes feed every lens, and My notes, expanded turns each one into what was said around it
  • Lenses: open any saved session in History and ask for a study guide, quiz, vocabulary list, action items, decisions, key points, or "what did I miss?" Fourteen lenses. Tick several, or a pack (Class pack, Meeting pack), and run them once; each result is saved with the session. Every item shows the lines it came from as Lines 212 to 231, tap to verify, and the transcript scrolls there
  • Quiz me, properly: eight questions in four types (multiple choice, multiple answer, true or false, short answer) with the answers hidden until you check them. Tap an option to commit before you look
  • On this phone or Cloud: every lens can run on the phone (free, private, works offline) or, if you turn Cloud lenses on in Settings, in the cloud for a deeper answer. Each result carries a badge saying where it ran. Nothing is sent until you choose Cloud for that session and confirm
  • What did I miss? the clock button in the live meeting header gives a quick catch-up of the last stretch without stopping the captions
  • Audio status chips: Bluetooth mic connected, too much noise, speaker may be too far, plus a Best Results guide for placement
  • Self-healing captions: phone call mid-session? Captions resume on their own. If recognition ever stalls, it restarts itself and tells you. It never fails silently

Everywhere

  • On your phone by default: captions, conversation, wrap-ups and lenses all work in airplane mode. Cloud lenses are optional and off until you turn them on
  • Personal Word Bank: teach it the names, jargon, and terms that matter to you, and captions learn to get them right
  • Fast ways in: home and lock screen widgets, Action Button, Siri, and Spotlight all launch straight into either mode
  • Made to be read: a new typeface designed for low-vision legibility, light and dark looks that follow your phone (or pick one under Appearance), full Dynamic Type support, VoiceOver labels throughout, and every animation respects Reduce Motion
  • One color language: orange is you (your replies, your notes, your name being mentioned), blue is incoming speech, teal means EchoSight is listening, ready, or showing you evidence, and red is kept for real problems
  • A PDF worth keeping: share any saved session as a clean EchoSight document: the wrap-up, every saved lens with its line references, your notes, and the numbered transcript with time stamps. Plain text is still there too
  • Your transcripts, your call: sessions save to History, browsable and exportable, and you decide what to keep

What to expect when you join

  1. An email from Apple arrives. The sender is "App Store Connect." That's the real invitation, not spam (though it sometimes lands in the spam or promotions folder, so check there).
  2. Install TestFlight. The invite asks you to download an app called TestFlight. That's Apple's own app for testing apps before they reach the App Store; every beta on iPhone goes through it.
  3. Accept the invite in TestFlight and EchoSight installs. From then on, updates arrive through TestFlight automatically, each with notes on what changed.
  4. First launch: allow microphone access, since captions can't happen without it. If your iPhone supports Apple Intelligence, turn it on (iPhone Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri): captions work without it, but the AI wrap-ups, lenses, and reply suggestions need it.
  5. Two minutes well spent: add your name and words you hear a lot (coworkers, jargon, medications) in EchoSight Settings → Word Bank. It genuinely improves accuracy.

Your personal iPhone is the right phone. EchoSight runs on the device and works offline, and nothing you caption leaves it unless you choose a Cloud lens for a session. No work-phone approvals needed; use it anywhere, including at work.

Sending feedback is two taps: take a screenshot in the app, tap the screenshot, then choose Share Beta Feedback. It comes straight to me with the context attached. Bigger thoughts belong in the wish list, and plain email or a text always works.

How to test: real scenarios

Pick the scenarios that match your life. You don't need to do all of them. One honest report from one real situation is worth more than an hour of artificial testing.

The lecture or long meeting

Start Meeting Mode for a class, staff meeting, church service, or even a long YouTube lecture. Let it run 30 minutes or more. Scroll back while it's still captioning, then jump back to live. When it ends, check the saved session and try the AI wrap-up.

Tell me: did captions keep up the whole time? Did anything stall? How was your battery afterward?

The study guide test

Record a class, a training session, or a long lecture video in Meeting Mode. Afterward, open it in History and tap Lenses. Tap Class pack and run it once. Open the study guide and tap Lines on a few items to watch where the transcript jumps. Then open Quiz me and answer a few before checking.

Tell me: did the evidence land on the right lines? Could you tell whether each lens ran on the phone or in the cloud? Did the study guide cover what you'd have written down yourself? Were the quiz answers really hidden until you checked, and were the questions fair to the lecture?

The note test

During a meeting, tap Add a note at this moment when something matters, type a few words while people keep talking, and save. Afterward, run My notes, expanded on that session.

Tell me: did the note land where you expected in the transcript, before or after the words that were said while you typed? Did captions keep going while you wrote? Did the expanded note say what was actually being discussed at that moment?

The cloud test

In Settings, turn on Cloud lenses and read How cloud lenses work. Then run one lens in the cloud on a long session and the same lens on the phone, and compare.

Tell me: was it clear what would be sent before you agreed? Did the wait feel acceptable for the length of the session? Was the cloud answer worth it compared with the phone's? Did you notice the badge saying where each result came from?

The light and dark test

Settings offers Appearance: System, Light, or Dark. Use Light in daylight and Dark in a dim room, in both modes, for at least one real session each.

Tell me: which one did you leave it on? Could you tell when EchoSight was listening? Could you tell why a line was highlighted? Was anything harder to read than before?

The PDF test

Open a saved session, tap Share, choose Share as PDF, and open it in Files or send it to yourself.

Tell me: could you find the wrap-up, the lenses, your notes, and the transcript without hunting? Do the line references in the lenses match the numbered lines below? Would you hand this to a classmate or a colleague as is?

The face-them test

In Conversation Mode, type a message and tap the flip button instead of Send. Hold the phone so the other person can read your side while you keep it in your hands. Then tap your text to flip it back and reply again.

Tell me: could they read it comfortably? Was flipping back obvious? Did you prefer this to handing the phone over?

The counter conversation

Use Conversation Mode somewhere real: ordering food, asking a store employee for help, a pharmacy pickup. Try the reply suggestions when they ask you a question, and try speak-aloud so the phone talks for you.

Tell me: were suggestions fast enough to actually use? Did they fit the question? Did the other person understand the spoken voice?

The interruption test

While captioning, have someone call you. Take the call, hang up, and watch whether captions come back on their own. Also try: switching to AirPods mid-session, letting the phone lock, and force-quitting the app mid-meeting (then check saved sessions; your transcript should be complete).

The name test

In Settings, enter your name plus any ways people mispronounce it or ways captions misspell it (the "also known as" field). Then have someone say your name across the room while Meeting Mode runs. Your phone should vibrate.

The vocabulary test

Settings has a Personal Word Bank. Add names and words captions keep getting wrong: your coworkers, your medications, your school's buildings, community terms. Then have someone use those words in conversation and see if captions improve.

Quick-launch tricks (try them all)

  • Widgets: touch and hold your home screen, tap the plus button, search "EchoSight." There are widgets for the home screen and the lock screen that launch straight into either mode
  • Action Button (iPhone 15 Pro and newer): Settings app, Action Button, choose Shortcut, pick "Start a Conversation" or "Start a Meeting." One press starts captioning from your pocket
  • Siri and Spotlight: "Start a conversation in EchoSight" or "Start a meeting in EchoSight" works from Siri, and both appear when you search in Spotlight
  • Quick phrases: in Conversation Mode, open the phrases panel. Install a scenario pack (Restaurant, Doctor and Pharmacy, Travel, Emergency and more). Send the same message three times and EchoSight offers to save it as a phrase
  • The hearing-person sheet: the person icon at the top of Conversation Mode gathers everything meant for the other person in one place
  • Appearance: Settings offers System, Light, and Dark. Light is dark ink on warm paper, which is where the old Sepia went. Try both in daylight
  • The testing card: Home shows what this build is testing, and ticks each item off as you do it. It lives on this phone only

Fixes to come: what I know about

An honest list. If you hit one of these, you don't need to report it (though a "me too" with a screenshot still helps me judge how much it hurts). If you hit something that isn't here, that's a report I need.

Known rough edges

  • The Meeting Mode tutorial is behind. Nothing it says is wrong, but it doesn't yet mention caption speed, the "hearing you" indicator, the audio status chips, lenses, or the Word Bank. The Conversation Mode tour was refreshed in 2.1; the Meeting tour is next. Until then, this page's feature tour is the complete list
  • Who said what can be wrong. Captions don't label speakers yet, so a wrap-up or lens can occasionally attribute a line to the wrong person, or read a question as a statement. Speaker labels are on the list; until then, check attribution against the transcript
  • Lenses would rather leave something out than make it up. An item with no supporting transcript lines is dropped, so a study guide or action-item list can come up shorter than you expected. If something obvious is missing, that's a report I want, with the session if you're willing to share it
  • Reply ideas can lose a fast exchange. At counter speed, suggestions sometimes arrive after the moment has passed, and "this or that?" questions don't always produce the actual choices as buttons. Both are being reworked
  • Distance has a ceiling. The recent room-tuning update helps, but a phone mic several meters from a quiet speaker is a physics problem. For big rooms, closer placement or an external mic is still the honest answer
  • On-device AI can be picky. Wrap-ups on everyday topics (health, religion, news) occasionally get declined by the phone's built-in model. You'll always get a clearly labeled basic extract instead of a dead end, but if you see it often, tell me
  • Cloud runs are capped at 20 a day per tester, and a pack of four lenses is four runs. Each run on a long session costs real money, which EchoSight pays during the beta. The count resets at midnight UTC. How cloud lenses work in Settings explains the rest
  • Quiz layout can drift. Once in a while the model ignores the question format and the quiz shows as plain question-and-answer text, answers visible. Run it again and it usually comes back right. A screenshot of a drifted one helps
  • Send versus speak is still a chip. The mode chip under Send stays for this build; separate Show and Say it buttons are next
  • Shake-to-clear for the composer is queued but not in yet

Being built next

Filed, specced, and in order. No dates promised, because I don't ship guesses.

  • Floating captions: keep captioning in a small window over other apps, including while it's tucked to the edge of the screen, so recalling it shows what's being said now, with nothing missed
  • Save & Start New: end one meeting and start the next without leaving the mode. Back-to-back classes, one tap
  • Silence markers: long quiet stretches marked in the transcript, so you can find the seams (speaker changes, breaks) when scrolling back. And honestly labeled: "quiet" and "interrupted" will never be confused
  • Multi-select delete: clear a whole run of old transcripts at once, with a strong confirmation and an undo window
  • Find anything later: search across all transcripts, folders, session renaming, and bookmarks you star live and jump to afterward
  • Focus marks: while a lecture is running, flag a term or topic you need explained. The study guide lens then defines it and gives it extra attention
  • Wrap-up layouts: choose what a shared wrap-up contains: full transcript plus summary, summary plus to-do list, or transcript only
  • Word Bank 2.0: teach it what a word keeps being misheard as, and let it suggest terms it noticed so the bank builds itself
  • Show and Say it: two explicit buttons in Conversation Mode instead of a send button plus a mode chip, so what you tap is what happens
  • Speaker labels: Speaker 1, Speaker 2, in color, once the phone can tell voices apart reliably. Until then, rails mark where a voice probably changed, never who
  • More scenario packs and iPad layouts

Further out (speaker labels, sound captions, translation) lives in the wish list conversation, and your input there genuinely steers it.

Privacy

Private by default. Nothing leaves your device unless you choose it, one session at a time.

EchoSight's captioning, speech, wrap-ups, and lenses run entirely on your iPhone. No account is needed, no network connection is required, and the app works in airplane mode. Your transcripts are stored only on your phone, you decide what to keep, and export happens only when you choose it.

EchoSight includes offline and local-only operation designed to support privacy-sensitive use. Whether use is appropriate in any specific setting, under HIPAA, FERPA, workplace, school, or clinical policies, remains the responsibility of users and their institutions.

Cloud lenses, plainly

Off by default. Cloud lenses do nothing until you turn them on in Settings, and even then nothing is sent until you choose Cloud for a specific session and answer a consent sheet for it.

What is sent: the transcript text of that one session, plus any notes you added to it. Never audio; EchoSight never records audio, so there is none to send. Where it goes: to EchoSight's own server, which checks that you are a signed-in tester (Sign in with Apple, which shares nothing but a random identifier), passes the text to a more capable language model, returns the result, and keeps no copy. The model provider is bound by terms that forbid training on your data. Providers are referred to by letter during the beta so results can be compared fairly.

What never goes: sessions in Compliance Mode, sessions that mention medical care, and sessions you chose not to save. Every cloud result carries a Cloud badge in the app and in the PDF, so you always know which parts of a record were made where.

Limits and cost: reading a three-hour transcript is tens of thousands of words for the model, and each run costs EchoSight real money. During the beta EchoSight pays, and each tester gets 20 cloud runs a day (a pack of four lenses is four runs). On-phone lenses will stay free after the beta; cloud lenses are expected to become part of a paid Enhanced plan with a monthly allowance. Nothing about that is final, and testers will hear first.

Deletion: the server stores nothing, so there is nothing on it to delete. Results are saved only on your phone, with the session, and go when you delete the session.
