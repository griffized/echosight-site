Coming to TestFlight Major update

Beta 2.1: ask more of a saved session

Why: a transcript is not the finish line. A 50-minute lecture is only useful if it can become a study guide, and a saved meeting is only useful if you can ask it questions afterward. Testers were clear about that. At the same time, two real exports showed the AI wrap-up doing something worse than missing things: it stated a number that was never said. This release takes on both.

What changed:

  • Lenses: open any saved session in History and ask for a study guide, a quiz, a vocabulary list, action items, decisions, key points, "what did I miss?", and more. Fourteen lenses in all. Every item shows the transcript lines it came from: tap Show evidence and the transcript scrolls to them, highlighted
  • What did I miss? during a live meeting, the clock button in the header gives a quick catch-up of the last stretch without stopping the captions
  • Honest wrap-ups: when audio quality was poor for much of a session, the wrap-up says so at the top, and action items and decisions are shown as said rather than reworded. Lines the phone couldn't hear well are marked [unclear] instead of guessed at
  • Numbers stay where they were said: a wrap-up now drops any figure that doesn't appear in the transcript in the sentence it came from, instead of rewording it. Fewer items, none invented
  • Face them: in Conversation Mode, one tap flips your side of the screen so the person across the table can read it without you turning the phone around. Tap your text to flip it back
  • Conversation tutorial refreshed: two sides of the screen, handing them the phone, send versus speak, and reply ideas are all covered now
  • Under the hood: saved sessions moved to a storage layout built for long sessions, so History stays quick as your transcripts pile up. Existing sessions migrate on first launch. Nothing to do on your end

Added in the second 2.1 build:

  • A clearer EchoSight: a new typeface designed for low-vision legibility, light and dark looks that follow your phone (Appearance: System, Light, Dark in Settings; Sepia became Light), and one color language: orange is you, blue is incoming speech, teal means listening, ready, or evidence, red is kept for real problems. Same modes, same buttons. A one-time sheet explains the four changes when you update
  • Notes at the moment: Add a note at this moment during a meeting lands your note in the transcript where you tapped, even if people kept talking while you typed. Touch and hold any line in a saved session to add a note after it. Notes feed the wrap-up and every lens; My notes, expanded now has something to expand
  • Lenses, several at once: tick lenses or a pack (Class pack, Meeting pack) and run them once. Each result saves as it lands. A first-open guide explains what lenses are, and each group says what it is for. Evidence reads as Lines 212 to 231, tap to verify everywhere
  • Cloud lenses: optional, off by default. Turn them on in Settings, then choose Cloud for a session to get a deeper answer from a more capable model. Transcript text only, never audio; the server keeps nothing; a badge on every result says where it ran. How cloud lenses work in Settings covers limits and cost: 20 runs a day per tester, each lens one run, EchoSight pays during the beta
  • Quiz me, properly: eight questions in four types with answers hidden until you check, and an answer key at the end of the PDF
  • Share as PDF: an EchoSight document with the wrap-up, every saved lens with line references, your notes, and the numbered transcript with time stamps and page numbers
  • Medical reminder: when a session sounds like a medical appointment, a banner reminds you that captions and summaries are not authoritative and never replace direct communication, including an ASL interpreter
  • What we are testing: a card on Home lists this build's four tasks and ticks them off as you do them. The build number now sits at the bottom of Home and Settings so screenshots say which build they came from

Lenses and wrap-ups need Apple Intelligence (iPhone 15 Pro and newer on iOS 26). Captions, Conversation Mode, Face them, and saved transcripts work on every supported iPhone.

Please test: the four tasks on the Home card, then the scenarios on the main page (the note test, the cloud test, the light and dark test, the PDF test). Beyond that: record a class or a long meeting, then open it in History and run the Study guide and Quiz lenses. Tap Show evidence on a few items and tell me whether it jumped to the right lines. Count how many items say "No supporting lines cited." If a wrap-up gets flagged for audio quality, tell me whether the flag felt fair for the room you were in. And if you spot a number in any summary that wasn't actually said, that's the report I most want.

August 6, 2026 Hotfix

Beta 2.0.2: hearing across the room

Why: testers reported weak pickup of conversation a few feet away. Part of that is physics, but part was us: meeting mode was running audio through phone-call-style noise filtering that can treat a distant speaker as background noise.

What changed:

  • Meeting mode now captures raw, unfiltered audio tuned for rooms, not phone calls. Conversation mode is unchanged
  • New "speaker may be too far away" indicator with a practical hint when distance is the problem
  • New "Big room?" section in the Best Results guide: Bluetooth clip-on mics, an AirPod placed near the speaker, moving an iPad closer
  • Honest limits, stated plainly: past roughly 4 to 5 meters in an untreated room, no phone mic keeps up. That's when an external mic wins

Please test: if captions felt weak from across a table or room before, try that exact situation again and tell me the difference. Distance reports with rough measurements ("about 10 feet") are especially useful.

August 6, 2026 Hotfix

Beta 2.0.1: captions, fast

Why: testers told us captions felt slower than other apps, and that quiet moments made it hard to trust the app was hearing anything at all. They were right.

What changed:

  • Captions now appear the moment words are recognized. Fast is the new default for everyone
  • New "hearing you" indicator: live sound-level bars show the app is picking up audio, even before any words appear
  • A "Getting ready" message covers the first seconds while the captioning engine starts up
  • Prefer the smoother, steadier display from 2.0? It's one switch away: Settings, Caption speed, Steady

Please test: does the app feel alive from the very first second? If you compared us to another captioning app before, please compare again and tell me which felt faster.

August 6, 2026 Major update

Beta 2.0: the reliability release

Why: a captioning app you can't trust to keep running isn't an accessibility tool. This release was several weeks of work on one theme: captions that never silently die.

What changed:

  • Captions survive interruptions: phone calls, Siri, and switching earbuds mid-session. They come back on their own
  • No silent failures: if transcription ever stalls, a banner appears and one tap restarts it
  • Crash-safe transcripts: even a crash or force-quit loses at most the sentence in progress. Everything else is already saved
  • Cleaner captions: duplicated sentences and stray one-word fragments are gone
  • Smarter reply suggestions: faster, matched to the question actually asked, and "this or that?" questions now offer the actual choices
  • Volume is yours: the automatic volume boost is removed. Helping with volume is now opt-in, with a visible slider, in Settings under Conversation audio
  • New voice-speed setting for spoken messages
  • Audio status chips in meeting mode: Bluetooth mic connected, background noise high, poor audio
  • A "Best Results" guide for device placement in rooms and classrooms

Please test: a real meeting or class of 30+ minutes, reply suggestions in a live conversation, earbud switching mid-session, and force-quitting mid-session (your transcript should be complete in saved sessions).

Earlier, 2026

Beta 1.x: the foundation

The first betas, tested with family and friends: live on-device captioning, Meeting Mode with full-session transcripts and AI wrap-up, Conversation Mode with the two-sided screen and speak-aloud, quick phrases with scenario packs, name alerts, personal word bank, saved sessions, widgets, Siri shortcuts, and the Action Button. Everything on the phone, nothing in the cloud. Those testers' reports shaped everything above.
